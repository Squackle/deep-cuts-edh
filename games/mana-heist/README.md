## What's new

Version 0.2 rebuilds the original abstract relay game around **tappable land cards outside the vault**, a visible mana pool, land drops, casting ward-breaking spells, life decisions and tight turn deadlines. The battlefield is a land row separate from the vault; tap a land to add exactly one of its possible colors and rotate it sideways. Tapped lands remain unavailable until you end the turn. The player must cast a sequence of named spells to open a vault.

Cards are **original land designs and names** inspired by familiar land *categories* rather than copied Magic: The Gathering card art, names, icons, or complete game rules. The land archetype names “basic”, “dual”, “shock”, “pain”, “check”, “fetch”, “bounce”, and “utility” describe what they do.

### How to play

- **Land drop:** One card may be played from hand each turn; existing battlefield cards begin untapped.
- **Tap:** Click or tap a battlefield card. Monocolor lands tap instantly; multiple-color cards offer a choice of exactly **one** mana color. The card visibly turns 90°.
- **Pool:** All produced mana goes into a visible, colored/lettered pool. It persists between actions and empties when ending the turn.
- **Cast:** Spend exact colored costs plus any colored/colorless payment for generic requirements. When generic payment is ambiguous, select a real allocation. Spell effects may let you choose and untap a land.
- **Land families:** Shock lands enter tapped unless you pay 2 life. Pain lands charge 1 life for colored mana but give colorless freely. Check lands depend on typed lands already in play. Fetch lands sacrifice themselves and charge 1 life to get a matching reserve land. Bounce lands enter tapped, returning another land to your hand. Duals tap for one of two colors.
- **Life:** Life can never reach 0. You lose only if an action would be illegal, but may always Undo or Reset. The level deadline forbids another end-turn action after its last turn.
- **Scoring:** Three stars when solved within the solver's fewest turns and least life spent among those fastest solutions; two for up to one extra turn (within deadline), one otherwise. Extra taps are **not** punished as clicks.

## 2.3 — Genuine Chromatic Pigment Mixing (2026-10-15)

Crucially, mobile was capped at ~30 FPS and desktop ~60 FPS, while velocity damping (`grid.drag`) and pigment turbulence/diffusion used once-per-frame multipliers. Thus mobile kept stronger flow longer. Fast desktop pointer motions also skipped intervening liquid, and the desktop Canvas looked comparatively soft when enlarged.

This focused **P12 partial polish** preserves the existing engine, 250 puzzle IDs, actual land cards, full-color pigment conservation and auto-cast. It:
- **Preserves the slow, satisfying mobile currents on BOTH devices:** velocity damping uses `Math.pow(drag, dt)` and a 60fps coefficient tuned to the old 30fps mobile lifetime; pigment diffusion and turbulence use `dt*0.5` (a 30fps reference). Physical transport accounts for the full elapsed time between frames; the old mobile 1.7-step clamp has been removed. A 60fps mouse and 30fps finger now follow comparable physical seconds rather than per-frame decay.
- **Smooths pointer input:** desktop hover stirs without a button and mobile touch-and-drag remains captured. Each pointer movement is resampled along the trajectory in steps of no more than 2.4 simulation cells, avoiding gaps when cursor reports sparse events. Impulse and rotary wake scale with the **distance traveled**, not arbitrary event counts; the mouse and touch gesture cover the same normalized fraction of the ward regardless of its displayed diameter.
- **Renders clearer on desktop:** the fluid solver remains a reusable 64×64 grid, but its Canvas backing is adaptively 256px mobile and up to 512px on desktop, with high-quality image interpolation. Rim glow and ripples are sized proportionally; resizing occurs when the game becomes visible and at throttled intervals. Mobile does not pay for the larger desktop bitmap.
- **Makes casting feel equally optional:** the 2-second initial window and **hard 5.1-second maximum** are intact, with a 900ms recent-gesture grace for hover and touch. Label is now device-neutral. Stopping interaction allows normal auto-cast; mixing is *never* required for puzzle success.

**Tests:** `tests/stir-parity.test.mjs` compares 60×1 frame steps with 30×2 frame steps for retained inertia, pigment mass balance and turbulence; simulates equivalent desktop hover and mobile touch paths with no mouse button, compares induced velocities and rendering density (512 vs 256). The existing 18 strict puzzle/auto-cast and all 250 certified level paths remain in the same GitHub CI. Browser preview verification includes desktop hover-to-stir with no click and mobile touch-and-drag, eventual automatic completion, readable hand/battlefield and no horizontal overflow. This is **not** completion of the entire P12 package (audio, full real-device/battery profiling, accessibility and further polish remain), nor a claim of physical device parity measured in a lab. Game Lab only; no public Deep Cuts EDH website or player data change.
