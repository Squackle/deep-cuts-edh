RESCUE REPORT 003 / THE SOLDIER RECRUITMENT DRIVE

Commander Mustard:
The most dangerous army is the one you overlook.

Mustard hands your Soldiers three keywords and turns combat into direct damage. You don't need a fancy combo. You need a lot of Soldiers that all attack at once.

01 / The diagnosis

Commander Mustard costs five mana, a lot for a commander who does not draw cards or make tokens himself. But the payoff is much stronger than his novelty art suggests: other Soldiers gain vigilance, trample and haste. For another four mana, Soldiers you control each gain an attack trigger that deals one damage to the defending player.

The mistake is to build Boros goodstuff and hope Mustard makes it better. He doesn't. He wants actual Soldiers—and as many bodies as your deck can afford. Mustard himself is a Soldier, so his activated ability can give him the attack-damage trigger, too, even though his passive keyword buff affects only the other Soldiers.

02 / The prescription

Build a recruitment pipeline, not a conventional creature curve. Cheap Soldiers establish the type line; instant-speed token makers let you rebuild; anthem effects make each body dangerous; and Mustard converts a wide battlefield into a stampede.

Ballyrush Banneret discounts your Soldiers. Preeminent Captain cheats a Soldier out of your hand when it attacks. Captain of the Watch brings three more Soldiers along. Myrel, Shield of Argive doubles down on troop production and makes it harder for opponents to intervene during your turn. None of these replace Mustard; they supply the army that gives his ability meaning.

The question isn't how big the Soldier is. It's how many of them can attack.

03 / The game plan

Early game / Raise a company

Open with two- and three-mana Soldiers, cost reducers and token spells. Deploy Marshal effects selectively; one large creature is less useful than four bodies with an anthem.

Midgame / Put Mustard in charge

Cast Mustard when your board already has a few Soldiers. The others gain haste, vigilance and trample, so newly cast Soldiers can attack immediately. Use removal or protection to keep your commander online through combat.

Late game / Count attackers, not size

When you can pay 2RW, activate Mustard before declaring attackers. Every Soldier that receives the granted ability and actually attacks triggers a point of direct damage. Two activations grant two separate triggers per attacking Soldier.

04 / Six pieces worth rescuing

These aren't the most expensive staples in the format. They're the cards that demonstrate why this particular commander works.

Ballyrush Banneret ↗

Discounting Soldier spells makes it easier to deploy multiple threats the turn before Mustard arrives.

Preeminent Captain ↗

Cheats a Soldier from hand into combat. That Soldier did not 'attack' for triggered-ability purposes, so don't count its Mustard ping that combat.

Myrel, Shield of Argive ↗

Protects your turn against many responses and creates Soldier tokens when she attacks. Those new tokens become your next wave.

Call the Coppercoats ↗

Instant-speed burst of 1/1 Human Soldiers based on a target opponent's creature count, perfect for replenishing after an opponent commits to the board.

Shared Animosity ↗

Every attacking Soldier gets bigger for the other attacking Soldiers sharing its type, making a wide board lethal fast.

Warleader's Call ↗

An anthem plus noncombat damage as creatures enter; protects your plan from needing every Soldier to connect in combat.

05 / Rules that matter

Attacking is an event. Creatures that enter already tapped and attacking—such as Soldiers put in with Preeminent Captain or created by certain attack triggers—were not declared attackers. They won't trigger a 'whenever this creature attacks' ability that combat.
Stack the activated ability. Mustard's activated ability gives Soldiers an attack trigger until end of turn. Activating it twice makes two separate triggers on each qualifying attacking Soldier. Wizards' 2024 release notes explicitly confirm this interaction.
Not every token is a Soldier. Cards that make generic Humans, Knights or Warriors do not qualify automatically. Read token type lines carefully: the relevant subtype is Soldier, not merely an army-themed illustration.

06 / What kills this plan?

  • Do not spend all your slots on five- and six-mana lords. The commander rewards critical mass, especially one- and two-mana bodies and token makers.
  • Protect the board against sweepers. Boros Charm and Unbreakable Formation are often better than another anthem when everyone knows you're going wide.
  • Pay attention to must-attack timing. The ability costs 2RW, so plan your mana before declaring attackers rather than assuming the ping is free.
  • Mustard gives other Soldiers haste and trample, not to himself. His own attack-trigger damage only comes from the activated ability.

Our verdict

Mustard looks like a joke commander until the table realizes five ordinary Soldiers can be ten attack-trigger damage before anyone blocks. The rescue is not an infinite combo. It's making lowly Soldier tokens collectively frightening.
