Budget Commander deck ideas.

Three overlooked Commander strategies with specific starter cards, honest tradeoffs and a practical checklist for turning an idea into an affordable 100-card deck.

01 / CHOOSE A PLAN

Which budget Commander strategy fits you?

The best starting point is a game plan you actually want to repeat, not a list of expensive staples you feel obligated to own.

RED / WHITEGO-WIDE COMBAT

Commander Mustard

Turn lots of modest Soldiers into a dangerous attacking force.

Core idea: Cheap Soldiers and token makers let Mustard's vigilance, trample and haste matter across the entire board. His activated ability turns each Soldier attack into more direct damage.

Cards to investigate: Ballyrush Banneret reduces Soldier costs, Preeminent Captain can put another Soldier into combat, and Captain of the Watch brings extra Soldiers.

Budget pressure: Repeated board wipes are painful, and the commander does not inherently refill your hand. Prioritize cheap repeatable card advantage and ways to rebuild before luxury anthems.

RED / GREENBIG MANA

Alena & Gilanra

Make a large creature, then turn its power into another spell.

Core idea: Alena, Kessig Trapper makes red mana based on the greatest power among creatures that entered under your control this turn. Gilanra, Caller of Wirewood helps with green mana and draws when her mana pays for a six-or-higher-mana spell.

Cards to investigate: Lupine Prototype offers five power at low mana cost; Phyrexian Soulgorger offers eight power with an upkeep drawback. Either can be a mana springboard if Alena can tap.

Budget pressure: Big-creature hands can stall when your commander or early ramp is missing. Pay for consistency, a sensible curve and protection before flashy finishers.

MONO-BLACKGRAVEYARD ENGINE

Chainer, Dementia Master

Use graveyards as a second hand, with a sacrifice outlet ready.

Core idea: Chainer can reanimate creatures from graveyards as Nightmares, but those Nightmares are exiled when he leaves. The rescue plan is to sacrifice valuable creatures in response to losing Chainer.

Cards to investigate: Viscera Seer and Carrion Feeder offer sacrifice outlets; Gray Merchant of Asphodel helps recover life spent on Chainer's ability.

Budget pressure: Black mana requirements, life payments and graveyard hate create real weaknesses. Stable lands, cheap sacrifice outlets and backup recursion matter more than the flashiest reanimation target.

02 / THE BUILD WORKSHEET

How to make a Commander deck cheaper without making it unplayable

Decide what you need the deck to do every game, then buy those functions before expensive upgrades.

  1. Set a real checkout ceiling

    Pick a budget—$50, $75 or $100 are useful planning examples—and decide whether it includes the commander, shipping, taxes, sleeves and lands. Record the cheapest available printing and condition you would actually purchase. These are sample budget limits, not verified deck prices.

  2. Reserve the boring cards first

    For a starting 100-card Commander deck, consider roughly 36–38 lands, around 10 ramp pieces, 8–12 card-advantage effects and 8–12 ways to interact. These are flexible planning ranges, not a universal formula: two partner commanders leave 98 other cards, while a single commander leaves 99. Tune your curve, colors and commander text rather than filling each category blindly.

  3. Buy your engine, not a generic pile of staples

    Mustard wants inexpensive Soldiers and repeatable token production; Alena wants power-efficient creatures and ways to untap or protect her; Chainer wants black mana, sacrifice outlets and graveyard access. If a card doesn't advance your plan or prevent it from failing, compare cheaper replacements before adding it.

  4. Compare printing prices and check legality

    Check current prices across actual stores or a deckbuilder; reprints and shipping can completely change a budget list. Confirm color identity, Commander legality and the format's current banned list. The Card Library helps discover cards, but its prices aren't a guaranteed checkout quote.

  5. Finish, goldfish and iterate

    Copy a Rescue Report starter package, fill remaining roles and reach exactly 100 cards including commander(s). Run sample opening hands, note how often you miss land drops or card draw, and replace the worst-performing pieces first. Try our deckbuilding resources and documented combo finder when checking the finished list.

03 / COMMON QUESTIONS

Before you buy the cards

Are these complete $100 Commander decks?

No. These are curated strategy ideas backed by existing, partial Rescue Report cores. No live merchant cart, printing selection or complete 100-card total has been independently verified. Treat $100 as a challenge to test, not a price claim.

What should I cut first when my Commander deck is over budget?

Start with premium versions of functional effects—expensive mana bases, splashy finishers and niche upgrades—rather than cutting all removal, lands or card draw. Revisit the deck's game plan after every change. A cheap card that solves a recurring problem can be worth more than a famous staple.

Which build is easiest to understand?

Commander Mustard has a straightforward battlefield goal: cast Soldiers and attack together. Alena and Gilanra reward turn sequencing and mana planning. Chainer asks you to manage graveyards, life and sacrifice timing. None of these is guaranteed strongest or cheapest; choose a play pattern you enjoy.

Why isn't Ekthi included here?

Our Ekthi preview is explicitly marked as not Commander-legal at the time of this guide. It's useful future brewing research, not a recommendation to purchase a playable deck today.
